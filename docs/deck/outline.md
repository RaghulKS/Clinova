## 1. Clinova

- Test-fly your NSCLC trial on thousands of virtual patients before enrolling a real one
- Every prediction is graded against how real ClinicalTrials.gov trials turned out
- https://github.com/RaghulKS/Clinova

**Visual:** Dark teal title slide; funnel and risk panels of the results screen (docs/screens/03-results.png, cropped) at right.

**Speaker notes (79 words):** Clinova is a flight simulator for non-small cell lung cancer trial protocols. A study designer pastes a draft protocol or an NCT ID, and Clinova shows where enrollment will stall and where patients will drop out, before the protocol is final. The difference from other tools is proof: every prediction is graded against how real ClinicalTrials.gov trials turned out, on trials the model never saw. Clinova advises the author; it never approves a protocol. Pitch wording: docs/ARCHITECTURE.md section 1.

**Sources:** docs/ARCHITECTURE.md

## 2. Most trial delays are written into the protocol before the first patient is screened

*The problem*

- 57% of protocols need a substantial amendment; median $535K each in Phase III
- Low accrual is the top reason cancer trials stop (34.5% of terminations)
- Clinova pulls the two levers the FDA cost study names: looser rules, simpler protocols

**Visual:** Three stat tiles: amendment rate, Phase III amendment cost, accrual share of terminations.

**Speaker notes (74 words):** Delays are designed in. 57% of protocols get a substantial amendment and 45% of those were avoidable (Starter Sheet 01). Each costs a median $141K in Phase II and $535K in Phase III plus about 3 months (Starter Sheet 01). Of 8,687 US cancer trials, 22.74% were terminated, and accrual was the top specific reason at 34.5% (PMC10028157). All figures: docs/ARCHITECTURE.md section 2. The FDA-commissioned cost study names looser enrollment rules and simpler protocols.

**Sources:** docs/ARCHITECTURE.md

## 3. Public version history and LLM parsing make protocol risk checkable today

*Why now*

- ClinicalTrials.gov keeps every registered version: amendments become public labels
- LLMs turn free-text eligibility into code-checkable rules
- Trial design is where AI adoption lags most (Tufts CSDD 2024)

**Visual:** Three numbered cards.

**Speaker notes (80 words):** Three things changed. First, ClinicalTrials.gov keeps every registered version of a protocol, so when a sponsor widens a rule, that edit is public. Starter Sheet 01 assumes amendment data is private; we use version history as a public proxy instead, so every claim can be checked. Second, an LLM can turn free-text eligibility into rules code can check. Third, the Tufts CSDD 2024 benchmark finds full AI adoption lowest in trial design and planning. Source: docs/ARCHITECTURE.md sections 2 and 3.

**Sources:** docs/ARCHITECTURE.md

## 4. Clinova test-flies a protocol in five steps, from draft text to a graded risk

*What Clinova does*

- Read, simulate, calibrate, optimize, learn
- The author sees where the funnel stalls, how likely failure is, and three fixes
- Every score is logged and graded when the real trial ends

**Visual:** Five-step process flow with numbered circles and arrows.

**Speaker notes (82 words):** Five steps. Read: Gemini turns eligibility rules and the visit schedule into code-checkable logic. Simulate: a virtual NSCLC cohort is screened in code, then persona agents decide whether to enroll and when they would drop out. Calibrate: LightGBM trained on real ClinicalTrials.gov trials gives calibrated risk, explained by SHAP. Optimize: test edits such as relaxing a criterion or cutting a visit, with ranges and a dollar value. Learn: every prediction is logged and scored when the trial ends. Source: docs/ARCHITECTURE.md section 1.

**Sources:** docs/ARCHITECTURE.md

## 8. Small, tested modules around one Tiger Data store, built entirely by AI agents

*Architecture*

- Parser, cohort screen, persona agents, LightGBM, optimizer and learning loop
- Public data only; MIT license; one-command Docker Compose with cached AI outputs
- Four orchestrators, two model families, 27 task prompts

**Visual:** Block diagram of the pipeline (ingest, parse, screen, agents, model, optimizer, loop, app) around Tiger Data.

**Speaker notes (79 words):** ClinicalTrials.gov records and their version history are ingested and labelled into Tiger Data, which is Postgres with TimescaleDB and pgvector. Gemini parses criteria; a code screen and persona agents simulate the cohort; LightGBM with SHAP scores risk; the optimizer re-scores every candidate edit in milliseconds; the learning loop logs and grades every prediction. Judges run it with one Docker Compose command and cached AI outputs. Four AI orchestrators across two model families ran 27 task prompts (count of orchestration/tasks/*.md).

## 11. We show what the evidence can and cannot support

*Limitations and responsible use*

- Registry edits are a public proxy for amendments; stop reasons are free text
- Personas are synthetic; predictions are correlational
- Clinova advises; the protocol author decides. No real patient data

**Visual:** 2x2 grid of limitation cards; teal banner with the responsible-use statement.

**Speaker notes (75 words):** Four limits. Registry edits are a public proxy for amendments, not the same thing. Stop reasons are free text, and some terminations are business decisions. Personas are general-population records given clinical traits from published distributions, labelled as assumptions. Predictions are correlational. On responsible use: no real patient data is used; Clinova advises the protocol author, logs every accept and reject, and never approves a protocol. Source: docs/ARCHITECTURE.md section 10 and the caveats in docs/metrics.json notes.

**Sources:** docs/ARCHITECTURE.md, docs/metrics.json
