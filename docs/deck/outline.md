## 1. Clinova test-flies an NSCLC protocol on virtual patients and grades itself on real trials

- Test-fly your NSCLC trial on thousands of virtual patients before enrolling a real one
- Every prediction is graded against how real ClinicalTrials.gov trials turned out
- https://github.com/RaghulKS/Clinova

**Visual:** Dark teal title slide; funnel panel of the app on the demo trial (docs/screens/03-funnel-risk.png, cropped) at right.

**Speaker notes (79 words):** Clinova is a flight simulator for non-small cell lung cancer trial protocols. A study designer pastes a draft protocol or an NCT ID, and Clinova shows where enrollment will stall and where patients will drop out, before the protocol is final. The difference from other tools is proof: every prediction is graded against how real ClinicalTrials.gov trials turned out, on trials the model never saw. Clinova advises the author; it never approves a protocol. Pitch wording: docs/ARCHITECTURE.md section 1.

## 3. Public version history and LLM parsing make protocol risk checkable today

*Why now*

- ClinicalTrials.gov keeps every registered version: amendments become public labels
- LLMs turn free-text eligibility into code-checkable rules
- Trial design is where AI adoption lags most (Tufts CSDD 2024)

**Visual:** Three numbered cards.

**Speaker notes (80 words):** Three things changed. First, ClinicalTrials.gov keeps every registered version of a protocol, so when a sponsor widens a rule, that edit is public. Second, an LLM can turn free-text eligibility into rules code can check. Third, the Tufts CSDD 2024 benchmark finds full AI adoption lowest in trial design and planning. ## 4. Clinova test-flies a protocol in five steps, from draft text to a graded risk

*What Clinova does*

- Read, simulate, calibrate, optimize, learn
- The author sees where the funnel stalls, how likely failure is, and three fixes
- Every score is logged and graded when the real trial ends

**Visual:** Five-step process flow with numbered circles and arrows.

**Speaker notes (81 words):** Five steps. Read: Gemini turns eligibility rules and the visit schedule into code-checkable logic. Simulate: a virtual NSCLC cohort is screened in code, then persona agents decide whether to enroll and when they would drop out. Calibrate: LightGBM trained on real ClinicalTrials.gov trials gives calibrated risk, explained by SHAP. Optimize: test edits such as relaxing a criterion or cutting a visit, with ranges across cohort seeds. Learn: every prediction is logged and scored when the trial ends. Source: docs/ARCHITECTURE.md section 1.

**Sources:** docs/ARCHITECTURE.md

## 8. Small, tested modules around one Tiger Data store, built entirely by AI agents

*Architecture*

- Parser, cohort screen, persona agents, LightGBM, optimizer and learning loop
- Public data only; MIT license; one-command Docker Compose with cached AI outputs
- Four orchestrators, two model families, 27 task prompts

**Visual:** Block diagram of the pipeline (ingest, parse, screen, agents, model, optimizer, loop, app) around Tiger Data.

**Speaker notes (79 words):** ClinicalTrials.gov records and their version history are ingested and labelled into Tiger Data, which is Postgres with TimescaleDB and pgvector. Gemini parses criteria; a code screen and persona agents simulate the cohort; LightGBM with SHAP scores risk; the optimizer re-scores every candidate edit in milliseconds; the learning loop logs and grades every prediction. Judges run it with one Docker Compose command and cached AI outputs. Four AI orchestrators across two model families ran 27 task prompts (count of orchestration/tasks/*.md).

## 10. In a 2020–2023 replay, monthly retraining did not beat the frozen model (Brier 0.078 vs 0.077) <!-- numbers: replay.summary.retrained_brier replay.summary.frozen_brier -->

*Learning loop*

- Replay 2020-01 to 2023-12: score each month, then learn its outcomes
- Pooled Brier over 1,736 overlapping windows: retrained 0.078, frozen 0.077 <!-- numbers: replay.summary.n_scored replay.summary.retrained_brier replay.summary.frozen_brier -->
- Every live prediction is logged in Tiger Data and scored when the trial ends

**Visual:** Cumulative Brier by month, retrained vs frozen (docs/deck/img/replay_brier.png, from docs/replay.json).

**Speaker notes (62 words):** The replay freezes a model on 2019-12-31, then steps month by month from 2020-01 to 2023-12: it scores trials whose outcome arrives within 6 months, then adds that month's outcomes and retrains. The frozen arm never retrains. Pooled over 1,736 scored windows the retrained Brier is 0.078 against 0.077 frozen. Windows overlap, so this is descriptive, not a significance test. ## 11. We show what the evidence can and cannot support

*Limitations and responsible use*

- Not shown to beat simple baselines; amendment labels are missing
- Personas are synthetic; scores are correlational
- Clinova advises; the protocol author decides. No real patient data

**Visual:** 2x2 grid of limitation cards; teal banner with the responsible-use statement.

**Speaker notes (85 words):** Four limits. Accrual AUC is above chance, but its paired gain over a logistic baseline includes zero. Version histories are not loaded yet, so hit rate and amendment risk are unmeasured. Clinical traits come from published distributions; too few simulated trials to test their benefit. They rank protocols against similar trials; they do not show that a rule caused a failure. Clinova uses no real patient data, advises the protocol author, logs every accept and reject, and never approves a protocol. Sources: docs/gate2_report.md, docs/metrics.json notes.

**Sources:** docs/gate2_report.md, docs/metrics.json
