## Themes

### Excessive Visit Frequency and Time Commitment

Participants find the requirement of fifteen visits spanning up to five hours each to be exhausting and incompatible with maintaining employment or managing daily life.

*Mitigation:* Consolidate stable maintenance visits and reduce in-person visit frequency during stable cycles.

### Travel Distance and Financial Toxicity

Living far from the study center or working hourly or service jobs creates severe logistical and financial barriers due to lost wages and lack of travel support.

*Mitigation:* Provide sponsor-backed travel stipends, transit services, and financial assistance for lost wages.

### Side Effect Concerns and Low Risk Tolerance

Patients, particularly those with low risk tolerance or living far from specialized care, worry about experiencing severe adverse events from the new oral drug.

*Mitigation:* Offer robust proactive side-effect management support and comprehensive on-site navigation resources.

### Geographic Isolation for Rural Participants

Rural participants face nearly impossible commutes over long distances multiple times a year without specialized local healthcare support.

*Mitigation:* Implement a hybrid model utilizing telehealth and partnering with local clinics for routine blood work.

## Site coordinators

**research coordinator at an urban academic cancer center with a dedicated trials unit**

Running this trial at our urban academic center will be challenging due to the heavy volume of fifteen visits over a year, which causes high attrition among working patients who struggle to take leave for frequent four-to-five-hour evaluations. Additionally, patients with low risk tolerance are hesitant about novel oral toxicities without guaranteed benefit. To improve retention, we suggest consolidating stable maintenance visits, offering flexible morning or evening appointment slots, and providing robust proactive side-effect management support.

- Consolidate stable maintenance visits
- Offer flexible appointment slots
- Provide proactive side-effect management support

**coordinator at a community oncology practice that runs a few industry trials a year**

At our community practice, patients face significant hurdles due to the sheer frequency of fifteen round-trip visits and the lack of explicit travel or cost reimbursements mentioned in the protocol. Many older or working patients find the time commitment exhausting, especially given the lengthy imaging and blood draw appointments. We recommend negotiating travel stipends with the sponsor, streamlining visit procedures to reduce chair time, and leveraging local laboratories for interim safety blood draws where feasible.

- Negotiate travel stipends with the sponsor
- Streamline visit procedures to reduce chair time
- Leverage local laboratories for interim safety blood draws

**nurse coordinator at a rural hospital oncology clinic, 1-2 hours from the nearest academic center**

Our rural patients find this protocol extremely difficult because driving over one hundred kilometers fifteen times a year for clinic visits and CT scans is practically unfeasible without transportation assistance. The substantial travel burden combined with fears of managing severe oral drug side effects far from specialized care leads to immediate refusal from eligible candidates. We suggest implementing a hybrid model with telehealth for select check-ins, partnering with local clinics for routine blood work, and offering dedicated travel reimbursement or transit services.

- Implement a hybrid model with telehealth
- Partner with local clinics for routine blood work
- Offer dedicated travel reimbursement or transit services

**coordinator at an urban safety-net hospital serving many low-income and uninsured patients**

Serving a low-income population makes retention exceptionally hard when patients hold inflexible service or hourly jobs and cannot afford lost wages across fifteen separate clinic visits. The absence of trial-funded travel support or compensation exacerbates financial toxicity, deterring vulnerable patients who might otherwise benefit from an oral therapy option. To address this, we suggest establishing sponsor-backed financial assistance for lost wages and transit, reducing in-person visit frequency during stable cycles, and providing comprehensive on-site navigation resources.

- Establish financial assistance for lost wages and transit
- Reduce in-person visit frequency during stable cycles
- Provide comprehensive on-site navigation resources
